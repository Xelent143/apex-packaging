## LinkedIn Post

Pallet packaging usually gets expensive when the first quote is built around a footprint only.

The details that change the result tend to come later:

1. The product weight and load shape were not defined clearly.
2. The pallet pattern and stack height were guessed instead of specified.
3. Stretch wrap was priced without corner boards, top sheets, or load-stability notes.
4. The route mixed warehouse storage and LTL handling without adjusting the spec.
5. Sample requirements were skipped even though the first load needed validation.

For a cleaner pallet-packaging RFQ, buyers should usually confirm:

1. Product dimensions, unit count per pallet, and total pallet weight.
2. Pallet footprint, stack height, and whether the load is column stacked or interlocked.
3. Stretch-film direction, gauge target, and whether machine or hand wrap applies.
4. Corner-board, top-sheet, cap, strapping, or label requirements.
5. Handling route: warehouse storage, internal movement, LTL, FTL, or mixed distribution.
6. Fragility, scuff risk, or movement risk inside the load.
7. Quantity, delivery timing, reorder cadence, and whether samples are needed.

That is how procurement teams compare pallet-packaging quotes by actual load performance instead of by first-pass material price.
