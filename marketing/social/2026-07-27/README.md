## LinkedIn Post

Printed packaging problems usually show up after artwork review, not before the first quote.

That happens when the buyer prices the box but leaves the print brief incomplete.

For printed packaging boxes, a stronger RFQ usually answers:

1. What structure is being quoted: corrugated shipper, folding carton, mailer, sleeve, or a mixed program?
2. What board grade, substrate, or film target should be quoted?
3. Does the job need one-colour flexo, digital print, litho-lam, or a more presentation-heavy finish set?
4. What brand colour reference should production match, and how tight does that match need to be?
5. Does the job need gloss, matte, soft-touch, varnish, foil, or another finish that changes cost and lead time?
6. Where do barcodes, compliance marks, or variable data have to sit on the pack?
7. Does the team need a sample or press proof before final approval?
8. What quantity, delivery route, and reorder pattern should the quote be built around?

That matters because a low first quote does not help if the coating changes later, the barcode zone gets revised, or the board needs to change after transit testing.
