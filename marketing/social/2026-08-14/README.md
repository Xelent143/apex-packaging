## LinkedIn Post

Kraft-box quotes usually become harder after the first visual review, not before the first price comes back.

That happens when the buyer sends dimensions but leaves the material and print brief loose.

For custom kraft boxes, a stronger RFQ usually answers:

1. What format is being quoted: retail carton, mailer, sleeve, gift box, or shipping carton?
2. What product dimensions, weight, and handling route should the structure be built around?
3. Which kraft tone or board target should be quoted, and how much print contrast does the brand need?
4. Does the job need black ink, white ink, labels, foil, or another finish that changes cost and lead time?
5. Are barcode zones, compliance copy, and front-panel priorities already defined?
6. Does the team need a sample to confirm fit, closure feel, and shelf presentation before production?
7. What quantity, launch date, reorder pattern, and delivery location should the quote assume?

That matters because a lower first quote does not help if the board changes later, the artwork loses contrast on press, or the sample stage reveals packing friction after approval.
