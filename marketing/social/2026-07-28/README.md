## LinkedIn Post

Low-MOQ packaging usually gets more expensive after the first quote, not before it.

That happens when the launch quantity is defined, but the product, route, sample needs, and artwork status are still vague.

For low-MOQ custom packaging, a stronger RFQ usually answers:

1. What product is being packed, and what are its exact dimensions and weight?
2. Is the first run a retail carton, branded mailer, corrugated shipper, or a mixed-format program?
3. Does the job need digital print, simple one-colour print, or a finish set that changes lead time?
4. Is artwork production-ready, or does the team still need dieline and prepress support?
5. Does the route involve parcel, courier, LTL, pallet, or retail handling?
6. Is a sample needed to confirm fit, closure, barcode zones, or presentation before production?
7. What reorder timing or repeat volume should the supplier keep in view from day one?
8. Are there material, sustainability, or compliance requirements that should be priced into the first run?

That matters because a low first quote does not help if the insert changes later, the board gets upgraded after transit review, or the reorder plan was never defined.
