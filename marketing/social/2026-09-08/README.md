## LinkedIn Post

Luxury packaging should feel premium at unboxing—but it also has to survive the route, pack efficiently, and reorder consistently.

Before comparing rigid boxes, premium folding cartons, or printed mailers, align the quote around:

1. Product dimensions, weight, fragility, and scuff-sensitive surfaces.
2. Retail, parcel, courier, LTL, or pallet distribution.
3. Insert fit, product removal, and the order of the unboxing experience.
4. Artwork status, colour references, foil areas, coating, and barcode zones.
5. Sampling for fit, closure, appearance, and packing friction.
6. Quantity, tooling, freight assumptions, lead time, and reorder frequency.
7. The same material, print coverage, finish, and included components across every quote.

Soft-touch, foil, embossing, and specialty papers can shape the presentation. They cannot compensate for a structure that moves the product, slows the packing line, or arrives with unclear specifications.

Start with the real product and route. Then choose the structure and finish that support both the customer experience and repeatable purchasing.
