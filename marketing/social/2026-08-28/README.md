## LinkedIn Post

Sustainable packaging is usually discussed as a material decision.

For procurement teams, it is really a specification decision.

FSC board, recycled content, PCR film, and kraft options only perform well when the packaging program is built around the actual product, shipping route, artwork scope, and approval process.

That means buyers should verify more than the sustainability claim itself:

1. The product dimensions, weight, and any fragile or scuff-sensitive surfaces.
2. The route, because parcel, pallet, courier, and retail handling change the structure and material mix.
3. The packaging format, whether that means corrugated, paperboard, poly film, inserts, or a mixed-format program.
4. The print and finish expectations, because coatings, colour control, and branding scope affect both cost and lead time.
5. The documentation path, including FSC status, recycled-content notes, and any internal procurement approvals.
6. The sample requirement, especially when fit, presentation, or handling performance still needs to be proven.
7. The reorder plan, so the approved sample becomes the benchmark instead of a one-off exception.

That is the difference between buying a sustainable-looking package and approving a sustainable packaging program that is actually repeatable.

The latest Apex guide walks through what buyers should verify before comparing quotes or releasing production.
