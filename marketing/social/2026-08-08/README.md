## LinkedIn Post

Custom box inserts usually get expensive when the first quote is built around a guess.

The material may look right on paper, but the real issues often show up later:

1. The product shifts or scuffs in transit.
2. The cavity is too tight for fast packout.
3. The retail presentation looks premium in renderings but not in the assembled carton.
4. The insert choice adds assembly labour that the team did not budget for.
5. The buyer compares quotes that were built around different protection assumptions.

For a stronger insert RFQ, the brief should usually confirm:

1. Product dimensions, weight, finish, and any fragile surfaces.
2. Shipping route: parcel, pallet, courier, retail, or mixed distribution.
3. Insert preference: corrugated, foam, molded pulp, or a supplier recommendation.
4. Presentation goal: simple protection, premium unboxing, retail face-up display, or multi-SKU organization.
5. Artwork status, barcode zones, and any print or labelling requirements.
6. Assembly-labour limits, packout speed targets, and whether pre-assembly matters.
7. Quantity, launch timing, reorder pattern, and sample needs.

That is the difference between choosing the cheapest insert and choosing the insert that protects the product, supports the brand, and still works on the packing line.
