## LinkedIn Post

Custom packaging quotes break down when multi-location teams compare unit price before they compare assumptions.

The avoidable misses usually look like this:

1. One location ships parcel and another ships pallet, but both were quoted to the same structure.
2. Artwork is still moving, yet the quote assumes production-ready files.
3. Samples were skipped even though the first run covers several destinations.
4. Quantities were shared without reorder timing, so MOQ and production logic were guessed.
5. Product dimensions were sent, but weight, fragility, and scuff risk were not.

For a cleaner custom-packaging RFQ, buyers should usually send:

1. Product dimensions, weight, and photos.
2. Packaging format target, or approval for Apex to recommend one.
3. Artwork status, print coverage, barcode zones, and finish notes.
4. Quantity by SKU, launch timing, and reorder frequency.
5. Delivery destination, carrier method, and shipping route.
6. Sample needs, approval process, and any sustainability or compliance notes.

That gives procurement teams a better way to compare corrugated, print, sampling, and lead-time tradeoffs before production starts.
