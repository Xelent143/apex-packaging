## LinkedIn Post

A custom box insert has two jobs: control the product and keep the pack-out repeatable.

The lowest insert unit price can still create the higher operating cost if it takes longer to assemble, allows the product to rotate, scuffs a finished surface, or requires extra void fill.

Before comparing corrugated, foam, and molded-pulp inserts, document:

1. Product dimensions, weight, centre of gravity, and acceptable movement.
2. Fragile features and every surface that cannot rub against the insert.
3. Parcel, courier, LTL, pallet, or retail-display route.
4. Required unboxing order and which face the customer sees first.
5. Operator steps: fold, load, orient, inspect, and close.
6. Quantity, reorder frequency, storage space, and approved substitutions.
7. Sample tests for fit, removal, retention, and outer-carton compatibility.

Corrugated inserts can fold flat and integrate with the carton. Foam can provide close cavity control for delicate or high-value items. Molded pulp can deliver repeatable geometry with a fiber-based presentation. The right answer depends on the product and operation.
