## LinkedIn Post

Luxury packaging usually gets expensive when the first quote is built around assumptions instead of a real specification.

The first issue is rarely the outside look alone.

It is usually one of these:

1. The finish scuffs in handling.
2. The insert fit slows assembly or feels loose in the unboxing.
3. The route requires more protection than the original concept allowed.
4. The buyer compares prices built around different structures, materials, or sample expectations.
5. The reorder plan was not defined, so the launch spec is hard to repeat consistently.

For a cleaner luxury-packaging RFQ, buyers should usually confirm:

1. Product dimensions, weight, surface sensitivity, and retail presentation goal.
2. Preferred format: rigid box, folding carton, premium mailer, sleeve, or open recommendation.
3. Finish target: soft-touch, foil, embossing, lamination, texture, or simple printed board.
4. Insert needs and whether packout speed matters on the line.
5. Artwork status, barcode zones, and approval notes.
6. Parcel, courier, pallet, retail, or mixed route.
7. Quantity, launch timing, reorder cadence, and sample requirements.

That is how premium packaging gets quoted around the real product instead of a generic visual idea.
