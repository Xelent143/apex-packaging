## LinkedIn Post

Printed packaging approvals tend to slow down when the quote starts before the print spec is actually defined.

The carton or mailer style may already be clear, but the real production path still depends on the product, route, material, artwork status, colour expectations, coatings, quantity, and whether a sample or proof is needed before release.

That is usually where cost and timing begin to move.

For a cleaner printed-packaging RFQ, it helps to define:

1. The exact product dimensions, weight, and any scuff-sensitive or presentation-critical surfaces.
2. The shipping route, because parcel, pallet, courier, and retail distribution do not ask the same thing from the structure or finish.
3. The material and format direction, including corrugated, folding carton, rigid, or mailer assumptions.
4. The artwork state, barcode placement, colour references, and coverage expectations.
5. The finish path, whether that means matte, gloss, soft-touch, foil, or other coatings.
6. The quantity plan, reorder timing, and whether the job includes one SKU or several.
7. Whether a proof, sample, or pre-production check is needed before full release.

That matters because a lower first quote is not a win if the coating changes later, the barcode zone conflicts with the artwork, or the approved colour target was never shared up front.

The latest Apex guide walks through how buyers can compare printed-packaging quotes more fairly and build a cleaner proofing path before production.
