## LinkedIn Post

Protective-packaging quotes usually break down after the first pack-out test, not before the first price comes back.

That happens when the RFQ covers dimensions but leaves the product risk and route vague.

For fragile product kits, a stronger quote request usually answers:

1. What product dimensions, weight, and fragile or scuff-sensitive areas should the package be built around?
2. Is the order shipping parcel, courier, LTL, pallet, or through a retail distribution mix?
3. Does the product need a foam insert, corrugated divider, molded pulp tray, void fill, or a combination?
4. Is the goal shock protection, cleaner presentation, faster pack-out, lower damage, or easier reordering?
5. Does the job need artwork control, barcode zones, or a structural review before the insert layout is approved?
6. Is a sample required to confirm fit, closure, and handling before production?
7. What quantity, launch timing, reorder pattern, and delivery destination should the quote assume?

That matters because a lower first quote does not help if the product rubs inside the carton, the void fill slows packing, or the sample stage reveals fit issues after approval.

The latest Apex guide walks through how buyers can define protective packaging around product risk, route, insert choice, sample planning, and reorder control before comparing price.
