## LinkedIn Post

Corrugated shipping box quotes usually break down after the first packing test, not before the first price comes back.

That happens when the RFQ covers dimensions but leaves the route, board target, and print brief vague.

For heavy ecommerce orders, a stronger quote request usually answers:

1. What product dimensions, weight, fragility, and surface risks should the carton be built around?
2. Is the order shipping parcel, courier, LTL, pallet, or through a retail distribution mix?
3. What board target, flute profile, and ECT range should the supplier quote against?
4. Does the job need one-colour print, label zones, barcode control, or a cleaner retail presentation?
5. Is a sample required to confirm fit, closure, and pack-out speed before production?
6. What quantity, launch timing, reorder pattern, and delivery destination should the quote assume?
7. Are there sustainability, compliance, or warehouse-handling notes that need to be priced in early?

That matters because a lower first quote does not help if the board grade changes later, the carton scuffs in transit, or the sample stage reveals packing friction after approval.
