## LinkedIn Post

Premium gift-box projects usually get more expensive after the first visual approval, not before it.

That happens when the outer box is defined, but the insert fit, wrap style, finish details, and shipping route are still unresolved.

For gift boxes, a stronger quote brief usually answers:

1. What products are inside the bundle, and what are their exact dimensions and weights?
2. Does the program need a rigid box, magnetic-close mailer, sleeve, wrap, or a mixed format?
3. Will the insert be foam, paperboard, corrugated, or molded pulp?
4. Does the package need foil, embossing, soft-touch, ribbon, or another finish that changes cost and lead time?
5. Is the unit going retail-only, parcel direct, or through a master shipper and pallet route?
6. Does the team need a sample to confirm insert fit, wrap tension, closure feel, and shelf presentation?
7. Are there recycled-content, FSC, or documentation requirements tied to the project?

That matters because a lower first quote does not help if the insert changes later, the finish is added after sign-off, or the route forces added protection.
