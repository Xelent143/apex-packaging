## LinkedIn Post

Tea packaging gets expensive after the first quote when the buyer only sends pack size and leaves the barrier, display, and route details for later.

That usually means the supplier still has to guess about aroma retention, carton versus tin versus pouch direction, artwork status, sample needs, and whether the pack is headed to ecommerce, shelf display, or a gift-ready multipack.

For a stronger tea-packaging RFQ, it helps to answer:

1. What is the exact tea format: loose leaf, sachets, tins, refill pouches, or a mixed gift set?
2. What fill weight, inner barrier level, and freshness target does the product need?
3. Is the format a shelf carton, a tin-and-carton set, a pouch, or a display-ready multipack?
4. Will the pack run through retail shelves, parcel shipping, wholesale cases, or all three?
5. Is artwork ready, or does the team still need dielines and print-prep support?
6. Does the launch need a physical sample to check fit, closure behavior, packing speed, and retail presentation before production?

That matters because a lower first quote does not help if the pouch loses shelf shape, the carton crushes in transit, or the display pack slows fulfillment.

The latest Apex guide walks through how buyers can define tea packaging around aroma protection, retail display, multipack structure, samples, and quote inputs before the order goes live.
