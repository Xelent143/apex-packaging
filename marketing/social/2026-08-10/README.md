## LinkedIn Post

Moving and storage boxes usually get expensive when the first quote is built around a size only.

The missing details tend to show up later:

1. The board grade is too light for the packed weight or stack height.
2. The hand holes are awkward under load.
3. The label area conflicts with seams, tape, or closure points.
4. The pallet pattern changes compression more than the buyer expected.
5. The reorder is hard to repeat because the approval notes were thin.

For a cleaner moving-box RFQ, buyers should usually confirm:

1. Internal dimensions, packed weight, and whether the contents are fragile, dense, or mixed.
2. Corrugated target: board grade, flute, single-wall or double-wall, or a supplier recommendation.
3. Carrying details such as hand holes, lids, closure style, and whether repeated handling matters.
4. Label zones, print expectations, barcode placement, and any room or contents fields.
5. Route details: office move, warehouse storage, courier, LTL, pallet, or mixed handling.
6. Quantity, launch timing, reorder cadence, and whether samples are needed.
7. Pallet pattern, stack height, and any wrap or edge-protection assumptions.

That is how buyers compare moving and storage boxes by actual performance instead of by first-pass unit price.
