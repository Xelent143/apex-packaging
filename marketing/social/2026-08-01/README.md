## LinkedIn Post

Industrial packaging problems usually start before production, not during it.

The quote gets built around incomplete assumptions.

For bulk and pallet shipments, a stronger RFQ usually answers:

1. What product is being packed, and what are the exact dimensions and weights?
2. Is the shipment moving parcel, LTL, FTL, or full pallet storage and release?
3. Does the load need Gaylords, liners, corrugated pads, edge protectors, stretch film, or a mixed system?
4. What stacking pattern, pallet height, and handling risk should the supplier design around?
5. Does the product need internal protection against scuffing, movement, or breakage?
6. What quantity, delivery destination, and reorder cadence should pricing reflect?
7. Are samples needed before the first production order?

That matters because a low first quote does not help if the board grade changes later, the liner was missed, the pallet wrap is underspecified, or the freight lane creates compression issues the original spec did not cover.

Industrial packaging gets easier to quote when the team sends the product and route details before asking for price.

Before requesting pricing, it helps to confirm:

- product dimensions, weight, and any fragile or scuff-sensitive areas
- Gaylord, corrugated shipper, liner, film, or mixed-format direction
- pallet pattern, stacking height, and shipping method
- material target, board grade, and protection needs
- sample requirements before first production
- quantity, destination, and reorder timing

That gives the packaging team enough context to recommend the right bulk-packaging setup before changes show up in freight, storage, or damage claims.
