## LinkedIn Post

Cosmetic packaging usually gets harder after the first quote, not before it.

That happens when the buyer sends dimensions but leaves out the finish, insert need, artwork status, or the actual shipping route.

For a retail launch, a stronger brief usually answers:

1. What product is going into the carton, and what are the exact dimensions and weight?
2. Does the pack need a carton only, a mailer plus insert, or a full shelf-ready display format?
3. Will the finish scuff, scratch, or show colour drift if print and coating are not defined early?
4. Is artwork ready, or does the team still need dieline and print-prep support?
5. Does the launch need physical samples to confirm fit, closure behavior, insert tolerance, and visual presentation before production?
6. Is the package moving parcel, pallet, retail distribution, or a mix of those routes?

That matters because a lower first quote does not help if the insert changes, the finish marks up, or the barcode zone shifts after approval.

The latest Apex guide walks through how buyers can define cosmetic packaging boxes around colour control, inserts, scuff risk, samples, and quote inputs before the first production run.
