## LinkedIn Post

Sustainable packaging usually gets harder to approve when the quote starts with a claim instead of a specification.

That happens when buyers ask for pricing on "eco-friendly packaging" but leave the material target, documentation requirement, route, or sample plan open.

For a stronger sustainable-packaging RFQ, the brief should usually answer:

1. What format is being quoted: corrugated shipper, retail carton, paper mailer, poly mailer, insert set, or a mixed program?
2. What route does the package need to survive: parcel, pallet, retail display, courier, or long-distance freight?
3. Is the priority FSC-certified fibre, recycled content, PCR film, lighter weight, curbside recyclability, or a balance of those?
4. What documentation has to be available for internal review, customer claims, or procurement sign-off?
5. Is artwork already approved, or does the structure still need dieline and print-prep support?
6. Does the team need a sample to check fit, closure, scuffing, shelf look, or barcode placement before production?
7. What quantity, reorder pattern, and delivery location should the quote be built around?

That matters because a lower first quote does not help if the board grade changes later, the recycled-content target is unsupported, or the package fails on the real shipping route.
