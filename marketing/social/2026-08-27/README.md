## LinkedIn Post

Low-MOQ packaging often looks simple until the first quote comes back and half of the production decisions are still missing.

A launch team may know the quantity, but the quote still depends on the product, route, structure, artwork state, sample path, and what the next reorder is supposed to look like.

That is where cost, lead time, and revision risk start to shift.

For a cleaner low-MOQ packaging RFQ, it helps to define:

1. The exact product dimensions, packed weight, and any fragile or scuff-sensitive surfaces.
2. Whether the first run needs a mailer, folding carton, corrugated shipper, or a mixed-format program.
3. The shipping route, because parcel, courier, pallet, and retail handling do not need the same structure.
4. Whether artwork is ready, or whether dieline, prepress, and design-support work are still part of the job.
5. The print path, from simple one-colour branding to digital full-colour presentation.
6. Whether a sample is required to confirm fit, barcode placement, closure performance, or shelf presentation.
7. The reorder plan, so approved materials and specs do not drift on the second run.

That matters because a low first quote is not really low if the board changes later, the insert fit fails, or the approved sample never became the benchmark.

The latest Apex guide walks through how buyers can quote low-MOQ custom packaging around the real product and the real approval path before production starts.
