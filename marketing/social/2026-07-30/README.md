## LinkedIn Post

Packaging design problems usually show up after the first review, not during the first quote.

That happens when the buyer prices the package before defining the real product, the route, the artwork state, or the sample plan.

For packaging design services, a stronger RFQ usually answers:

1. What format is being quoted: corrugated shipper, folding carton, mailer, insert set, rigid box, or a mixed program?
2. What are the exact product dimensions, weight, and any fragile or scuff-sensitive areas?
3. What route does the package need to survive: parcel, pallet, LTL, courier, retail display, or a mixed flow?
4. Does the quote need a specific material target, board grade, insert style, or closure method?
5. Is artwork already prepared, or does the job still need dieline and print-prep support?
6. Do barcode zones, compliance copy, or front-panel priorities need to be built into the structure review?
7. Does the team need a sample before production to confirm fit, appearance, closure, or packing speed?
8. What quantity, launch timing, reorder pattern, and delivery destination should the quote be built around?

That matters because a lower first quote does not help if the structure changes later, the artwork still needs rework, or the sample reveals a packing issue after approval.
